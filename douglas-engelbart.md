# Douglas Engelbart

Author of [Augmenting Human Intellect: A
Conceptual Framework](Sources.md) (Stanford Research Institute, Summary Report AFOSR-3223, October
1962), the document that gives this corpus its framework and its missing lineage link.
Writing from SRI under Air Force Office of Scientific Research contract AF 49(638)-1024,
Project 3578. In this wiki he matters less as a person than as the author who read
[Vannevar Bush](vannevar-bush.md) closely, quoted him at length, and then argued the [Memex](memex.md) was a
*primitive* case of something more general.

## What matters here

**He had built the thing.** §III.A.3 of the report describes an edge-notched card system
*"that I developed and used over the past eight years"* — kernels of thought on cards,
one deck per problem area, needle-sort retrieval, source number and page at the top of
every card. Then its failure, in his own words: he *"just didn't have the means to keep
track of all of the kernel statements… and the various relationships between them"* by
means easy enough to leave capacity for the actual thinking. That is
[Maintenance Burden](maintenance-burden.md) reported first-person by the person who coined the framework, and
it is the strongest evidence in this corpus. → [H-LAM/T](h-lam-t.md), [Symbol Structuring](symbol-structuring.md)

**He repositioned Bush rather than repeating him.** Bush's trails are *"a beautiful
example of a new capability in symbol structuring"* — and *"a primitive example"* of
arbitrary substructure definition. The memex's real contribution, on Engelbart's reading,
is crossing a **usability threshold**: with ordinary equipment the same work was possible
but *"his mental time constants of memory and patience would have rendered the system
unusable."* → [Memex](memex.md), [Associative Indexing](associative-indexing.md)

**He confirmed Bush's trails are social.** §III.A.2 singles out trail duplication and
gifting, *"wholly new forms of encyclopedia"*, the profession of [trail
blazers](trail-blazer.md), and inheritance *"including the entire scaffolding"* — reading them as
*"changes in the ways in which people can cooperate intellectually."* This independently
corroborates the C-002 ruling against [LLM Wiki — A pattern for building personal knowledge bases using LLMs](Sources.md)'s claim that
Bush's vision was private. → Contradictions

**He supplied the lineage this corpus was missing.** On Newell, Shaw and Simon's list
structures: *"a string of substructures that are linked serially in exactly the manner
proposed by Bush for the associative trails in his Memex."* Bush → list processing →
Engelbart, sourced rather than asserted.

## Limits of this page

Everything here comes from **one document, written by its own subject**. There is no
independent biographical source in this corpus — no dates, no education, no career
history, and nothing about what happened after 1962. In particular, the 1968
demonstration of the oNLine System and the subsequent history of his research programme
are **absent**, which means this wiki currently knows Engelbart's *proposal* and nothing
about whether it worked. Deliberately not filled from memory, per AGENTS §3.
`confidence: medium` for that reason.

Self-authored sources also carry an obvious bias: the report argues for funding a research
programme its author would lead. §VI is an *"open plea to researchers and to those who
ultimately motivate, finance, or direct them."* Read the confidence accordingly.

## Open questions

- Did the framework survive contact with the actual NLS system? The report proposes;
  1968 onwards answers. Not in this corpus.
- Were typed links ever evaluated quantitatively? §III.B.8 reports cleaner structures and
  fewer members, with no numbers — and §III.B is fiction.
- What became of the "trail blazer" idea? He names it approvingly and never returns to it.

# Intelligence Amplification

The goal [Douglas Engelbart](douglas-engelbart.md) adopted for his programme, and the crucial claim about
*where* the resulting intelligence lives: **in the system, not in the person.** *"What
possesses the amplified intelligence is the resulting H-LAM/T system, in which the LAM/T
augmentation means represent the amplifier of the human's intelligence"*
([Augmenting Human Intellect](Sources.md), §II.C.2).

## Definition

The term was not his. He records that it was *"jokingly suggested several times during
the course of this study"* and attributes it to W. Ross Ashby. His first reaction was to
reject it: *"on the grounds that in our view one's only hope was to make a better match
between existing human intelligence and the problems to be tackled, rather than in making
man more intelligent."* He then changed his mind, because [Synergism](synergism.md) supplied the
missing warrant:

> Accepting the term "intelligence amplification" does not imply any attempt to increase
> native human intelligence. … We will have amplified the intelligence of the human by
> organizing his intellectual capabilities into higher levels of synergistic structuring.

Three distinctions are load-bearing:

1. **Amplification ≠ increase.** Native intelligence is untouched. What changes is the
   organization of the whole.
2. **The bearer is the system.** Not the human, not the artifact — [H-LAM/T](h-lam-t.md).
3. **It is continuous with history, not a rupture.** *"In a very real sense, as
   represented by the steady evolution of our augmentation means, the development of
   'artificial intelligence' has been going on for centuries."* Writing, notation, tables
   and filing were all amplifiers.

## Why it matters

**It dissolves the "does the tool make me dumber" question as posed.** If intelligence is
a property of the human-plus-artifact system, then asking whether an LLM agent degrades
its owner's intellect is asking the wrong question — the relevant question is whether the
*system's* capability rose. This bears directly on an open worry in this vault
(Open Questions: *"does owner understanding atrophy when the agent does all
synthesis?"*). Engelbart's framework does not answer it, but it reframes it: atrophy of
the human component is only a loss if the system's capability falls with it.

Which is not reassurance. A system whose human component stops understanding the corpus
has lost its ability to **direct** — and direction is the part
[Repetitive vs Creative Thought](repetitive-vs-creative-thought.md) says cannot be mechanized. The framework makes the
risk precise rather than removing it.

**It sets the bar for evaluating a knowledge base.** Not "is the wiki well organized?"
but "is the owner-plus-wiki system more capable than it was?" That is Engelbart's minimal
measurement requirement (§IV.F): *"(a) knowing when an improvement in effectiveness has
been achieved, and (b) knowing how to assign relative value to the changes derived from
two competing innovations."* This vault currently satisfies neither.

## Criticisms and limits

- **He adopted the term on an argument, not on evidence.** The warrant is
  [Synergism](synergism.md), which is itself an analogy. The chain is plausible at every step and
  measured nowhere.
- **"Amplification" implies a quantity that is never defined.** Amplifiers have gain.
  Nothing here could tell you whether gain is 1.2× or 10×, and the one number in the
  report is from the **fictional** §III.B (AGENTS §6.8).
- **The continuity claim may understate the discontinuity.** Writing and an LLM agent
  differ in that the agent *generates* rather than *records*. Whether that is a
  difference in degree or kind is exactly what the framework's vocabulary cannot express.
- **It is a funding document's concept.** §VI is *"an open plea to researchers and to
  those who ultimately motivate, finance, or direct them."* A term that makes the
  programme sound continuous with centuries of progress is rhetorically useful.

# H-LAM/T

[Douglas Engelbart](douglas-engelbart.md)'s name for the unit of analysis in augmentation work: *"Human using
Language, Artifacts, Methodology, in which he is Trained"*
([Augmenting Human Intellect](Sources.md), §II.A). The claim
is that intellectual effectiveness is a property of this four-part **system**, not of the
person or of any tool they use — so improving it is a systems-engineering problem, and
evaluating a tool in isolation is a category error.

## Definition

Four **augmentation means**, all of which must be considered together (§II.A):

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **L — Language** | *"the way in which the individual parcels out the picture of his world into the concepts that his mind uses to model that world, and the symbols that he attaches to those concepts"* |
| **A — Artifacts** | *"physical objects designed to provide for human comfort, for the manipulation of things or materials, and for the manipulation of symbols"* |
| **M — Methodology** | *"the methods, procedures, strategies, etc., with which an individual organizes his goal-centered (problem-solving) activity"* |
| **T — Training** | *"the conditioning needed by the human being to bring his skills in using Means 1, 2, and 3 to the point where they are operationally effective"* |

The human's capabilities are already extended by all four: *"Individuals who operate
effectively in our culture have already been considerably 'augmented'"* (§II.B). His
thought experiment is that an aborigine with identical native capabilities cannot drive
through traffic, request a library book, or call a committee meeting — the gap is
entirely LAM/T.

**Dynamic interdependence** is the point. §V: *"The elements are dynamically interdependent
within an operating system."* A change in any one propagates: *"a new process capability
appearing anywhere within that hierarchy can make practical a new consideration of latent
change possibilities in many other parts of the hierarchy."*

## Capability repertoire hierarchy

The system's capabilities form a hierarchy (§II.C.5). Every process decomposes into
sub-processes, terminating in **basic capabilities** — those that cannot be usefully
changed further. Three kinds: *explicit-human*, *explicit-artifact*, and *composite*
(both). Engelbart's analogy: *"This repertoire is like a tool kit, and just as the
mechanic must know what his tools can do and how to use them, so the intellectual worker
must know the capabilities of his tools and have good methods, strategies, and rules of
thumb for making use of them."*

The design consequence: humans take only *"little steps"*, and augmentation means exist to
decompose large problems so those steps suffice. *"The human mind neither learns nor acts
by large leaps, but by steps organized or structured so that each one depends upon
previous steps."*

## Why it matters

**It explains why tools alone fail.** Engelbart is explicit: *"It is plain that even if
the equipment (artifacts) appeared on the market tomorrow, a good deal of empirical
research would be needed to develop a methodology that would capitalize upon the artifact
process capabilities."* Artifact without Methodology and Training yields little. That is
the strongest available argument for a written schema governing a knowledge base — the
schema *is* the M, and it does not come with the software.

**It locates this vault precisely.** An Obsidian vault plus an LLM agent is not an
artifact; it is an H-LAM/T system. `AGENTS.md` is the Methodology. The wikilink
conventions, the page templates and the citation rules are Language. The collaborative
ingest ritual is Training. Judging the system by the editor's features misses three of
four components.

**It reframes the division of labor.** [Repetitive vs Creative Thought](repetitive-vs-creative-thought.md) asks which
tasks to delegate. H-LAM/T asks a better question: which *system* results? See
[Intelligence Amplification](intelligence-amplification.md) — the amplified intelligence belongs to the whole, not to
the human component.

## Criticisms and limits

- **Unfalsifiable as stated.** "The system is what matters" is hard to disagree with and
  hard to test. Engelbart concedes the framework *"is still rudimentary"* (§I.B) and that
  *"no existing analytic theory is by itself adequate"* (§II.B), recommending experimental
  methods instead.
- **T is the weakest component, and the least examined.** Training appears mostly as an
  assumption. The report's only concrete claim about it — fifteen hours producing
  *"significant and personally thrilling"* capability in non-specialists — sits in §III.B,
  which is **fiction** (AGENTS §6.8).
- **It can license tool-blame.** If effectiveness is systemic, a failed system has no
  single cause, which is true but also conveniently unfalsifiable for anyone selling a
  component.
- **The four categories may not be exhaustive or disjoint.** Language and Methodology
  overlap heavily; artifacts embed methodology. Engelbart himself says he is *"not sure
  how the structuring concept must mature for our needs."*

# Vannevar Bush

Author of [As We May Think](Sources.md) (*The Atlantic Monthly*, July
1945), in which he specified the [Memex](memex.md) and [associative
indexing](associative-indexing.md). At the time of writing he was Director of the Office of Scientific Research
and Development (OSRD), where, per the journal's editor's note, he *"has coordinated the
activities of some six thousand leading American scientists in the application of
science to warfare."*

## What matters here

Bush wrote the essay as a **redirect argument**: the war is ending, so what should
scientists do next? His answer is that they should turn to *"the massive task of making
more accessible our bewildering store of knowledge"* (editor's note). That framing
matters for reading him correctly. He is not designing a personal productivity tool; he
is proposing a national research priority, and the memex is the illustrative endpoint of
a survey that begins with photocells, microfilm, and machine arithmetic.

Two of his arguments carry directly into this vault:

**1. Machines should take repetitive thought, not creative thought.** *"For mature
thought there is no mechanical substitute. But creative thought and essentially
repetitive thought are very different things. For the latter there are, and may be,
powerful mechanical aids"* (§3). And in §4: the creative element *"is concerned only with
the selection of the data and the process to be employed, and the manipulation
thereafter is repetitive in nature and hence a fit matter to be relegated to the
machines."* This is the [Repetitive vs Creative Thought](repetitive-vs-creative-thought.md) distinction, and it is the
same division of labor [LLM Wiki Pattern](llm-wiki-pattern.md) proposes 81 years later — human curates and
selects, machine does the bookkeeping.

**2. Ideas fail on economics, not on soundness.** Leibnitz's calculating machine
*"embodied most of the essential features of recent keyboard devices, but it could not
then come into use. The economics of the situation were against it"* (§1). Babbage's
engine: *"His idea was sound enough, but construction and maintenance costs were then too
heavy."* Bush's generalization — *"The world has arrived at an age of cheap complex
devices of great reliability; and something is bound to come of it"* — is structurally
the same move [LLM Wiki — A pattern for building personal knowledge bases using LLMs](Sources.md) makes about the memex itself. See
[Maintenance Burden](maintenance-burden.md).

## Views and interpretations

> [!warning] How this corpus mischaracterized him
> [LLM Wiki — A pattern for building personal knowledge bases using LLMs](Sources.md) says of Bush: *"The part he couldn't solve was who
> does the maintenance. The LLM handles that."* Bush did address it — he proposes
> *"a new profession of [trail blazers](trail-blazer.md)"* (§8). He also describes a system
> whose trails are shared, gifted, and inherited, which contradicts the pattern doc's
> characterization of his vision as *"private."* Both are recorded in Contradictions
> (C-001, C-002) and corrected on [Memex](memex.md) and [Maintenance Burden](maintenance-burden.md).

The accurate version of the pattern doc's claim: Bush identified the labor requirement
and proposed staffing it with a profession. What he did not do — what nobody could in
1945 — was make trail-building cheap enough for one person to do alone at useful volume.
That is the gap an LLM fills, and it is a narrower and more interesting claim than
"he couldn't solve it."

## Limits of this page

Everything here comes from **one document**: the essay plus its editor's note. This wiki
has no independent biographical source for Bush — no dates, no education, no other work,
no account of his influence on Engelbart or Nelson. Those are deliberately absent rather
than filled from memory, per AGENTS.md §3. Recorded in Open Questions.

Confidence is `medium` for that reason: the primary text is solid, but a single
self-published-in-a-magazine source is a thin basis for a person page.

## Open questions

- What was Bush's actual influence? [Trail blazers](trail-blazer.md), [Memex](memex.md), and
  associative indexing are usually cited as precursors to hypertext — but this corpus
  contains no source making or testing that claim.
- Did he ever return to these ideas after 1945? Not in this corpus.
- Was the trail-blazer profession meant as a serious labor-market proposal or a
  rhetorical flourish? It gets one sentence.

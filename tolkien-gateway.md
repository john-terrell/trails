# Tolkien Gateway

A fan wiki covering Tolkien's legendarium, cited by
[the pattern doc](Sources.md) as the model of what a rich,
densely interlinked knowledge base looks like — and as evidence that such things do get
built and maintained.

## What the corpus actually says

> Think of fan wikis like Tolkien Gateway — thousands of interlinked pages covering
> characters, places, events, languages, built by a community of volunteers over years.
> You could build something like that personally as you read, with the LLM doing all
> the cross-referencing and maintenance.
> — [LLM Wiki](Sources.md)

It appears in the "reading a book" use case: file each chapter as you go, build pages
for characters, themes, and plot threads, and end with a companion wiki.

## Why it is doing real argumentative work

Tolkien Gateway is the strongest evidence in the source, and the doc doesn't quite
claim credit for it. Fan wikis demonstrate three things at once:

1. **Dense interlinking is achievable** at thousands of pages — so the shape is not
   utopian.
2. **People will do the [bookkeeping](maintenance-burden.md)** when the subject matters
   enough to them — so the obstacle is cost, not impossibility.
3. **Distribution solves it too.** A community spreads the burden across many
   maintainers. The [LLM Wiki Pattern](llm-wiki-pattern.md)'s move is to replace the community with one
   agent — which is why the doc's phrasing is "build something like that *personally*."

That third point is the interesting one and is worth remembering: the pattern is not
the only answer to maintenance burden, just the one that works for a single person.
